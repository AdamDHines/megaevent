Nine runs, one lever that mattered

Nine runs, 20 000 annealed steps each, one axis changed per arm, scored on three benchmarks. Read against a one-point floor, most of the ablation grid is noise, the sim-to-real frontier is still there and now measured directly — and the checkpoint that should ship is the plain ViT-B anchor that had no ablation on it at all.

Verdict

05

What was actually run

Nine 20 000-step runs, all sharing the Phase-1/2 fixes: annealed cosine LR (no --stop-after), the channel-exact PNG re-render, evenly-sampled normalisation stats, cph+sf held out of training and used as the in-domain monitor, MS loss at the canonical (1.0, 0.0), fp32 loss, and full P=32 batch on ViT-B. One axis changes per arm.

06

The anti-correlation survived annealing

The plan's central bet was that the LR never coming down was what made real-event recall decay while in-domain recall climbed. It wasn't. With the LR annealed to zero, eight of nine runs still peak on Brisbane inside the first 2 500 steps and then fall for the remaining 90 % of training, while the in-domain monitor rises the whole way.

07

The learning rate moves you along the frontier, not through it

Per-condition, step 2 000 → step 20 000

The decay is not spread evenly. In every other run it is daytime and sunrise that collapse — the two conditions whose illumination is furthest from the sunset traverse the old selection criterion was reading. A1 is the only run that gains on all four.

08

Scoreboard at the annealed endpoint

With the LR annealed to ~0, step 20 000 is the converged model for every run — the same step for everyone, no checkpoint selection involved. This is the fair cross-arm comparison, and it is the one to read if you want a model you can ship without gambling on a milestone.

Against v1, on identical query and reference sets

09

What survives a one-point floor

A7 repeated the ViT-S anchor at seed 1 and nothing else, which is the only direct calibration in the sweep for how much of an "effect" is just a different random draw. It came back at 0.9 points on Brisbane and 1.2 on the in-domain monitor at the endpoint — so a one-point floor is not a conservative convention here, it is roughly what the sweep measured about itself.

Arm by arm

Each row is measured against its own backbone: the ViT-S arms against the mean of the two ViT-S seeds, A1 against the ViT-B anchor. Greyed numbers sit inside their benchmark's floor and carry no claim. The seed pair is the caveat on all of it — one pair estimates a spread, not a distribution, and it can only bound the ViT-S arms. There is no ViT-B seed twin at all, so A1's row leans on all three of its numbers being large and consistently signed, and on the trajectory evidence in §3, rather than on any single endpoint difference.
