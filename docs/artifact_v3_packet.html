v3 — only the levers that survived, plus the one never tried

  
Six 20 000-step ViT-B runs and two 2-hour FixRes probes, launched 2026-08-01 23:52.
  v3 revisits nothing that came back under the floor: no ScanNet chunking, no domain randomisation,
  no XBM, no LLRD. It tests the three levers with a measured above-floor effect — negatives,
  capacity, learning rate — and the one question the whole project had never touched, training
  resolution.

  
None of the six had finished. Every number here is read off the
  checkpoints mirrored to local disk, which stop at 2026-08-02 09:29 — between step 5 000 and
  step 10 000 of 20 000, depending on the arm. So every comparison below is step-matched:
  each arm against the v2 ViT-B anchor at the same step of the same schedule, never against its
  annealed endpoint. Comparing a mid-anneal model to a converged one would flatter the anchor by
  exactly the amount the anneal is worth.

09
What was actually run

  
Nine 20 000-step runs, all sharing the Phase-1/2 fixes: annealed cosine LR (no
  --stop-after), the channel-exact PNG re-render, evenly-sampled normalisation stats,
  cph+sf held out of training and used as the in-domain monitor, MS loss at
  the canonical (1.0, 0.0), fp32 loss, and full P=32 batch on ViT-B. One axis changes per arm.

  





  
10
The anti-correlation survived annealing

  
The plan's central bet was that the LR never coming down was what made real-event
  recall decay while in-domain recall climbed. It wasn't. With the LR annealed to zero, eight of nine
  runs still peak on Brisbane inside the first 2 500 steps and then fall for the remaining 90 % of
  training, while the in-domain monitor rises the whole way.

  
  






  
11
The learning rate moves you along the frontier, not through it

  

  
  
Per-condition, step 2 000 → step 20 000

  
The decay is not spread evenly. In every other run it is daytime and sunrise that
  collapse — the two conditions whose illumination is furthest from the sunset traverse the old selection
  criterion was reading. A1 is the only run that gains on all four.

  





  
12
Scoreboard at the annealed endpoint

  
With the LR annealed to ~0, step 20 000 is the converged model for every run — the same
  step for everyone, no checkpoint selection involved. This is the fair cross-arm comparison, and it is

13
What survives a one-point floor

  
A7 repeated the ViT-S anchor at seed 1 and nothing else, which is the only direct
  calibration in the sweep for how much of an "effect" is just a different random draw. It came back
  at 0.9 points on Brisbane and 1.2 on the in-domain monitor at the endpoint — so a one-point floor is
  not a conservative convention here, it is roughly what the sweep measured about itself.

  
  
Arm by arm

  

  
Each row is measured against its own backbone: the ViT-S arms against the
  mean of the two ViT-S seeds, A1 against the ViT-B anchor. Greyed numbers sit inside their
  benchmark's floor and carry no claim. The seed pair is the caveat on all of it — one pair estimates
  a spread, not a distribution, and it can only bound the ViT-S arms. There is no ViT-B seed twin at
  all, so A1's row leans on all three of its numbers being large and consistently signed, and on the
  trajectory evidence in §11, rather than on any single endpoint difference.
