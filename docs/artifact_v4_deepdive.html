The MegaEvent Audit

Under corrected protocols the fine-tuned model's single-stage lead over the best baseline was +0.004 R@1. The audit found no fatal bug — it found a mechanism, three broken benchmarks, and one unclaimed advantage. With the second stage built from our own tokens, MegaEvent now leads Brisbane by +0.037 best-vs-best and trails MegaLoc on NSAVP by 0.005. The project is salvageable, and the salvage is already running.

1 · What the benchmarks were actually measuring

Finding. No single published number was wrong, but three protocol choices each moved a conclusion: NYC's query split, the native-only descriptor space, and the I2E provenance of half the datasets.

NYC-Event was near-duplicate retrieval

The published protocol draws 10% of samples as queries at random across all 15 recording sessions, so 99.8% of queries keep a same-session neighbour in the gallery a median 1.81 m away. Holding each session out — the same measurement Brisbane and NSAVP already make — collapses every method by ~0.7 R@1. One session is a disjoint route with zero scorable queries. The built-in cross-check reproduced every published cell from the same cached banks before scoring, so this is the same data under an honest split.

The normalization asymmetry cut both ways: countmask-matched statistics lift MegaLoc by ~3 points but hurt MixVPR (0.872 → 0.861 whitened) — model-dependent, so each control is reported in its own best configuration above.

The I2E benchmarks are in-domain, vignette-controlled

Training is ~4.77M I2E-simulated frames of MegaLoc's own corpus, so Tokyo/Pitts share the generator and MSLS the source imagery. On the co-recorded Brisbane arms, our margin over Event-GeM grows real→synthetic by +0.055 R@1, and the vignette-masked control strengthens it to +0.083 — the domain advantage is real and survives the control. The +0.05..0.09 I2E leads over MegaLoc stand, labeled as in-domain results. Two further labels the paper needs: MSLS is the publicly recoverable subset (36,207×1,094), and the "pitts" set is same-panorama view retrieval, not Pitts30k.

2 · Why MegaLoc reads events

Mechanism, measured. Events reach an RGB model as a noisy edge rendering of the scene. Nothing about the countmask encoding is doing hidden work — and the benchmarks' hard axes reward MegaLoc's RGB-learned invariances, not event-domain knowledge.

CKA between the photo and event representations is only 0.38 while top-1 retrieval agrees 52% of the time — the transfer lives at the metric level, not in shared features. MixVPR (a 10.9M ResNet) being competitive rules out a "large ViT reads anything" story.

The recorded-set gap is one axis: heading

NSAVP's pooled gallery is 64% the same road driven backwards, so its score is mostly "how often do you pick a same-heading frame". MegaLoc's forward-pick rate is 0.652; RGB-SALAD 0.603; ours 0.568; our earlier v4 0.523 — monotone in how much event fine-tuning a model has had. Three measurements close the loop:

  • The heading-partitioned SF-XL streams are in training — and their loss is the slowest to descend, still ~0.90 at the step-3500 shipping point. The heading content is under-trained exactly where real-event selection stops the run.
  • A cross-condition linear probe decodes heading from MegaLoc/SALAD descriptors at 0.627/0.644 but from every fine-tuned model at 0.509–0.564: fine-tuning erodes heading information the RGB backbone had.
  • The trajectory audit shows the frontier is monotone across the whole run: Brisbane peaks at step 500–1000 (0.886 whitened — better than the shipped 0.874) and decays to 0.864 by step 10k, while heading selectivity and I2E fit move the other way. Selection can pick a point on this frontier; only training changes can move it.

brisbane_v5/trajectory_p*.json · scratchpad heading probe over nsavp_pooled banks · wandb output.log per-stream losses

Brisbane's other axis, illumination, is one events remove at the sensor: every model fails night identically (R@1 ≤ 0.10 in all directions) and a query's score is set by its best illumination partner in the gallery — no headroom for event-domain knowledge there either. The benchmarks, as a set, barely test what an event model could be better at.

4 · The salvage: two-stage MegaEvent

The unclaimed advantage. Event-GeM's homography rerank was worth ~20 R@1 and we had no second stage — while our encoder computes 529 patch tokens per frame and throws their geometry away. Re-ranking the top-50 with mutual-NN token matching + RANSAC (Event-GeM's own verification model, its own defaults) claims it.

scripts/megaevent_rerank.py · rerank/<dataset>/rerank_*.json · top-50, RANSAC 5.0, w=0.05, min-activity 0.1

One diagnosis was load-bearing: with all 529 tokens, any two DAVIS frames "verify" against each other on the static bonnet and dead vignette, and the rerank degrades Brisbane by −0.08. Masking event-inactive patches (<10% active pixels — an event-native signal no RGB method has) fixes it. The mask threshold was set once from that diagnosis; a sensitivity sweep over the two knobs is running and will be reported as sensitivity, not used to pick a maximum.

Honest caveats: the prototype re-ranks at ~2.8 queries/s single-threaded against Event-GeM's optimized 22 ms/query — an engineering pass is required before latency claims; and the single-stage latency story (ViT-S encodes in 7.8 ms, full compute 16.3 ms vs Event-GeM's 30.3 ms) only carries to the two-stage system after that pass.

5 · Verdict

Salvageable — and the claim has changed shape.

The criteria set before the experiments: clear Event-GeM's 0.877 Brisbane cell and flip NSAVP, or the contribution is the recipe study and benchmark corrections. Where that landed:

  • Brisbane: cleared. 0.914 vs 0.877, best-vs-best, with every baseline given its best space and its best normalization.
  • NSAVP: not flipped, but the −0.078 became −0.005, and the residual is a named, trained-for quantity (heading selectivity) with two funded attacks: the v7 arms now queued, and heading-hard negative sampling designed for the wave after.
  • NYC: void for everyone. Its corrected form is a sparse-overlap cross-season benchmark on which no current method works — worth reporting as such.

What the paper can honestly claim: a two-stage event-native system (global descriptor + event-activity-masked token verification) that beats every published baseline on the recorded benchmark where matching is possible, ties the strongest RGB model where heading selectivity decides, and wins its training domain outright; plus the protocol corrections, which are a contribution on their own. What it cannot claim: that single-stage event fine-tuning beats RGB models at place recognition — the mechanism section is the reason, and pretending otherwise would not survive review.

The planned EVK4 collection is where the thesis gets its real test: every benchmark here is heading-and-illumination constrained; none varies viewpoint or rotation, which is where an event representation should earn its keep.

6 · Roadmap

Submitted: the v7 wave (gept/run_v7.sh)

  1. b_v7_evalres — selection at 322 alone; the trajectory audit predicts its pick moves.
  2. b_v7_nohflip_chunk — chirality + ScanNet hygiene.
  3. b_v7_xbm — cross-stream negatives with re-minted labels (the confounded v2 retest).
  4. b_v7_P96 — the unsaturated negative-pool trend.
  5. s_v7_nohflip_chunk — the deployable ViT-S mirror.

Judge on Brisbane/NSAVP best-space and the NSAVP forward-pick rate. After v7 reports: heading-hard negative sampling (design in gept/docs/report_2026-08-19_deepdive.md), the rerank engineering pass, and multi-window descriptor fusion (the remaining event-native capability nothing here has claimed yet).

EVK4 dataset design, informed by every failure mode above

  • Heading-aware ground truth from day one (position + bearing), so heading selectivity is scored, not confounded — the NSAVP lesson.
  • Viewpoint and in-plane rotation grids at fixed places — the axis no current event benchmark varies and the collection's reason to exist.
  • Repeated traverses per condition so leave-one-out protocols exist from the start — the NYC lesson.
  • Hardware-synchronized clock for camera and RTK/IMU — the Brisbane 7-second lesson.
  • Paired RGB capture co-mounted, so mechanism controls (photo / edges / events) come free — the section-2 lesson.
  • Night with measured event density, and stationary segments labeled — the density floor at which matching fails is itself a result.
